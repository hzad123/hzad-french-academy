## 3. The final questions requested for special attention

### Test 05 Q39 — B: time horizon, area and carbon accounting

The company’s announcement claims that the preceding year’s **100 tonnes** have been neutralized. The technical forecast concerns **10 tonnes per hectare accumulated from planting to year 20**, across **10 hectares**. The reader must keep the area multiplication, annual emissions and cumulative future absorption separate. The arithmetic yields the advertised 100-tonne figure, but the storage is a future modelled result, not a completed result for the past financial year.

All four options describe credible reporting errors in parallel language: confusing an annual flow with an eventual stock; bringing a twenty-year projection into the past year; confusing per-hectare and project totals; or attributing reference-plot carbon to the new planting. Only the time-shift mechanism matches the passage. The observed reference trees support the model rather than supply the project’s credited area, and the text explicitly multiplies by project surface. Replacement plants and insurance do not advance the date when storage occurs. The question does not rely on a visibly more cautious or comprehensive answer.

### Test 06 Q39 — B: quality and the composition of actual attendance

The smaller groups produce longer exchanges and more precise accounts of what visitors discovered. At the same time, **first visits decrease while repeat visits stay stable**, so the share of first visits falls. New names on the initial booking list do not establish that more new people ultimately attend: some places are taken by regular visitors when the early-confirmation system does not work for people with variable schedules.

Each option accepts the improvement in exchange quality but proposes a different account of who attends. A adds unestablished increased diversity; C invents a parallel decrease in repeat visits; D treats new registrations as increased realized first attendance. B reconciles quality, replacements, first visits and stable repeats. The passage supports the conclusion about attendance composition without claiming that the second trial has already proved a solution.

## 4. Independent review method and what the checks mean

1. **Inspect the question and four options without the passage.** Record whether a student could guess from answer length, grammatical fit, an obviously moderate tone, an implausible alternative or a generic “best policy” answer. The aim is four credible alternatives before the source is read.
2. **Read the actual passage, stem and all four options.** Select the best-supported answer before viewing the stored key, explanation or regression expectation. Identify exact source evidence and the textual reason each competing answer fails. The owner-provided B for Test 01 Q30 is the disclosed exception to complete prior-key ignorance.
3. **Freeze the source-based selection.** The recorded decision is the basis for later key comparison. Rewrites are reviewed again against their revised passage and options; selected repairs receive a fresh reader as well.
4. **Compare the stored key and explanation.** Check that the letter points to the intended exact option, the explanation supports that meaning, and its rejection of the other three options does not invent facts or overstate causation.
5. **Bind deterministic tests to the completed record.** The fixture includes a hash of the full question, including the explanation. A later textual edit invalidates the acceptance until review is updated. Tests check all four scoring alternatives for each advanced item and version-preserving behavior in a local database.

The resulting record covers **90 selections, 270 rejected alternatives and 90 explanations**. Every Q35–39 review contains at least two actual source excerpts and a stated need to combine details. The quality judgments address causes, consequences, conditions, scope, sequence, competing interpretations and the limits of the reported evidence. The full option analysis remains available in the fixture; the appendix below gives a readable source/answer/demand index.

This work uses direct editorial inspection and existing deterministic local validation. **No external AI API requirement or AI review pipeline was added.** Automated checks can detect a stale fixture, a changed key, misplaced options or a migration that affects the wrong records. They cannot independently establish that an educational interpretation is correct or that a distractor will challenge actual students.
